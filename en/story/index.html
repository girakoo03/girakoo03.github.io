01
History · Local · Spirit

Beyond the name of a place,
we look for the spirit it holds.

We visit historic places, listen to their stories, and record them in scent with a contemporary sensibility.

For BALLO, heritage lives in a place’s history, its local character and its enduring spirit. Ganghwa Anglican Church was the first place SILENT BLEND set out to remember in scent. There, traditional Korean hanok architecture meets a basilica plan, and symbols from different traditions share the same ground. In the way they come together, we found consideration and respect.

Our early project, “Local History Perfume,” began with a question: which stories of a place could we carry in scent? We wanted the fragrance and its design to grow from those stories, so that the connection to a place would reach beyond its name.

02
Through the gates

Through the gates,
different traditions meet.

The approach to the church passes through two traditional sammun gateways: the outer gate, then the inner gate. At the entrance, a cross, a taegeuk symbol and a bell shaped like a traditional temple bell bring familiar forms into a new setting.

As Christianity took root here, familiar forms of gates and bells became part of the church. Passing from the outer gate to the inner gate, we encounter its architecture and symbols in turn.

03
Hanok & basilica

A basilica within a hanok.
One space, with room for difference.

Outside, roof tiles, eaves and timber give the church the form of a traditional Korean hanok. Inside, a long central space, side aisles and a lofty interior reveal a basilica plan. Each architectural tradition retains its character within the same building.

Wood, light
& quiet presence.

Traditional lattice windows and arches, columns and timber joints invite a closer look. Light from high windows reveals the grain of the wood. The seating, cross and religious statues show that this remains a living place of worship.

The story of the two trees inspired the woody character of SILENT BLEND. That scent also recalls the timber and light we encountered here.

04
Meanings intertwined

Dragon heads and the twelve disciples.
One form, layered meanings.

From a distance, we see architecture; up close, we read its symbols.

Dragon heads · Twelve disciples

In traditional architecture, dragon heads have been associated with warding off evil spirits and protecting a place. Our brand book connects the twelve dragon heads here with Jesus’ twelve disciples. For us, two traditions meet in this single form.

Fish · Good fortune

Our brand book sees two traditions in the fish ornaments at the eaves: familiar wishes for abundance and good fortune, alongside Christian symbolism. The same form invites more than one reading.

Church · Ark

The church can also be read as an ark of salvation, bringing its architecture into conversation with faith.

From eaves to label ↓

These interpretations draw on the BALLO brand book.

05
Rooted together

Two trees planted together.
A fragrance born of that spirit.

At Ganghwa Anglican Church, a story is told of two trees from different traditions planted side by side. Within the grounds of a Christian church, both were given a place to take root. For BALLO, that choice speaks of consideration and respect.

CONFUCIUSScholar tree

A tree symbolising Confucius and Confucianism

BUDDHABodhi tree

A tree symbolising the Buddha and Buddhism

Different traditions arrived in one place and took root together. The journey and the act of planting the trees side by side inspired the woody character of SILENT BLEND.

The original scholar tree was later brought down by a typhoon. Yet the decision to plant the two together still matters to us, and we remember the spirit of that choice.

A choice to share the same ground.
A story remembered in scent.

07
From place to form

European ornament,
with an echo of mother-of-pearl.

The lines of Ganghwa mugwort leaves repeat in a pattern inspired by European ornament. Printed in fine lines and tonal contrasts, it recalls mother-of-pearl while bringing a local motif into BALLO’s own aesthetic.

The mugwort and pattern images come from the motif studies in our brand book; the photograph on the right shows the product box. Mugwort is used here as a design motif, not as a claim about the product’s ingredients.
